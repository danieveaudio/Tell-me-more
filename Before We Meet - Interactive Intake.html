Question 1 of 12

What does a really good Tuesday look like in two years?

Not aspirational. Not perfect. Just: actually good. What time do you wake up? What are you doing? Who’s around? How does your body feel?

If the image won’t come: don’t picture two years from now. Picture one perfect ordinary morning from your current life. What was different about it? Start there.

Perfect Day Prompt

If the image won’t come, try this: don’t picture two years from now. Picture one perfect ordinary morning from your current life — a morning that felt right.

Finish these: “The last time I woke up and felt genuinely okay, it was ___.” / “What made that morning different was ___.” / “The thing I was looking forward to that day was ___.”

That’s your signal. That’s the thing worth building toward.

Question 3 of 12

What do people come to you for — even when you haven’t offered it?

The thing friends ask your opinion on. The problem that lands in your lap at parties. The thing you find yourself explaining or helping with, unpaid and unbidden.

This question is often where the thread lives. People who don’t know what they want to build almost always already know how to help — they just haven’t named it yet.

Strengths Inventory Exercise

Most people’s real strengths are invisible to them because they feel effortless. That ease is not a sign they don’t matter. It’s a sign you’ve internalized something most people haven’t.

Try this: Name three times in the last year when someone thanked you and meant it. What were you actually doing for them? What specifically did they say?

Write those three moments. The pattern across them is usually the thread.

Also: the Red/Green Energy Audit in Work in the Wild is a 3-day exercise that tracks what charges vs. drains you. The green things are almost always adjacent to the answer.

What’s Already True
Question 4 of 12

What’s working? What have you built, created, or figured out so far?

This can be tiny. A system. A client you loved. A piece of content that landed. Knowing what you don’t want. All of it counts.

This might be skills from your job, relationships you’ve built, ways you show up that people notice. Things you’re quietly proud of even if they don’t feel like business assets yet.

The Invisible Asset Inventory

When nothing feels like it’s working, it’s usually because the real assets aren’t visible yet — not because they don’t exist.

List 10 things you’ve done in the last 12 months that you’re quietly proud of. Not big wins. Things you figured out, things that were harder than they looked, things you survived.

At least three of those are business assets. Write them and we’ll figure out which ones in session.

Question 5 of 12

What’s not working? What have you tried that hasn’t moved things?

Include everything — courses, coaches, programs. If you’ve invested in something that didn’t land, I want to know. It tells me a lot about where you actually are.

On Misaligned Investment

If things didn’t work, the problem is almost never you. It’s usually a mismatch between what was offered and where you actually were.

For each thing that didn’t work: What did you go in hoping to get? What did you actually get? That gap is often the clearest signal of what you actually need. Write it above — it tells me more than the list of what you tried.

Question 6 of 12

What are you walking away from — and what made it wrong?

Most people arrive at this work in the middle of a transition. There’s often grief in that — even when the thing wasn’t right, even when leaving was your choice. Knowing what you’re done with helps us build something that doesn’t recreate it.

On Transition and Identity Loss

What you’re feeling — the fog, the confusion, the grief that doesn’t quite make sense because you chose this — is a documented experience. Herminia Ibarra’s research on career reinvention describes it: “Change always takes longer than we expect because to make room for the new, we have to get rid of some of the old selves we are still dragging around.”

The confusion is not a sign you’re doing it wrong. It’s a sign you’re doing something real.

You don’t have to have left yet. Just write what you know you’re done with. That clarity is the starting point, not the finish line.

Question 7 of 12

Who do you want to help? Even a fuzzy answer is useful.

A type of person. An industry. A life stage. A feeling someone is in when they come to you. You don’t need a niche statement — just a direction.

“Honestly, people like me” is a completely valid starting answer.

Three Ways to Find Your Audience

Your nervous system often knows before your brain does.

The coffee test: Who would you have coffee with for free, just because the conversation would be interesting? What are they working on or struggling with?

The protection test: Who do you find yourself defending when other people don’t get them? That protectiveness usually points at your audience.

The mirror test: Who were you three years ago, before you figured something out? The person you were before is often exactly who you’re meant to serve.

Question 8 of 12

What do you absolutely not want to do, even if it ‘works’ for other people?

No videos? No cold DMs? Weekends protected? Build the edges before you build the structure.

Question 9 of 12

What does your energy look like on a real week?

How many hours can you give to building right now? What time of day do you work best? What do you need around you to think clearly?

Capacity Audit

Irregular energy isn’t a problem to fix before you start. It’s a constraint to design around. Every plan we build needs to fit your actual capacity, not an imaginary version of you who has it together.

Quick map: Rate your typical energy in each part of the day (morning / afternoon / evening) 1–3. Then: what are the 2–3 hours per week where you’re most likely to be sharp? That’s your building window. Write it above. We’ll design your plan around it.

The Click You’re Looking For
Question 10 of 12

If one thing got clearer in our conversation, what would it be?

This is your win condition. Orient around it and the whole session earns itself.

The Magic Wand Prompt

Finish this: “If I woke up tomorrow and one thing had magically resolved itself, it would be ___.”

Don’t overthink it. The first thing that came to mind before you could edit it — that’s usually the real answer.

The Financial Picture
Question 11 of 12

What does this need to do for you, financially?

Not your bank balance. Just: what are we solving for?

On Money and What You’re Allowed to Want

That stomach response is useful information, not a problem to fix. Most people — especially people who care deeply about the work — have a complicated relationship with charging for it. The Trauma of Money framework names this well: our money stories are often inherited, not chosen.

One question: If you knew the number you wanted was completely reasonable and you were allowed to want it — what would it be? You don’t have to share it. Just let yourself know it. Then write whatever you’re comfortable with above.
